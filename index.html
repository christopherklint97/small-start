    A BETTER WAY THROUGH

    Small shifts, real support.

    01Lower the starting line+

    Turn “finish the project” into a visible first action. If it still feels big, start with two minutes. CHADD ↗

    02Design out distractions+

    Silence nonessential notifications, move the phone away, or use headphones. Changing your environment can be easier than resisting every cue. NICE ↗

    03Make breaks less sticky+

    Try water, stretching, or a short walk rather than an infinite feed. Shorter focus periods with movement breaks are one possible environmental adjustment—not a universal prescription. NICE ↗

    04Return without shame+

    Write down the detour and restart with one small action. Support strategies can help, but a timer is not ADHD treatment. NIMH ↗

    05Give stimulating activities an exit+

    Before opening a game or feed, decide what “done” looks like and set a device-level limit. Keep the next offline action visible. This is a practical experiment, not a proven detox method.

    THE THINKING BEHIND IT

    Support, not pressure.

    Specific next actions and an external place for thoughts draw on CHADD’s adult ADHD planning advice. Flexible shorter work periods, movement breaks, and fewer environmental distractions are examples in NICE ADHD guidance. NIMH describes established treatment options; this app is a self-management aid, not a substitute for care.

    The 2-minute start, 60-second pause, and Pomodoro intervals are optional design choices. They are not proven ADHD-specific doses, and this app cannot block other sites or apps. Use your device’s notification and app-limit settings if you want stronger boundaries.
